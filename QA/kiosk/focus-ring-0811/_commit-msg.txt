kiosk (35): the console cancelled the focus ring and replaced it with 1.06:1

Every contrast run in QA/kiosk so far graded the console at rest — what a colour
is when nothing is focused — and not one of them pressed Tab. Both rules that
decide what a keyboard user sees opened with `outline: none`, and what stood in
for the browser's ring was a border-colour swap to `--accent`. Measured for the
first time here: 1.60:1 in light, and 1.06:1 in dark. The second is not a faint
indicator, it is no indicator. Every field on every screen, the login form
included. WCAG 2.4.7 is Level A.

`--focus-ring` is deliberately not one value. Light is `--accent` itself, 5.28:1
on the white card. Dark is `--accent-control`, the lightened blue already chosen
for controls in dark, at 7.12:1 — `--accent` there sits against the *paler* dark
border `#61708f` and gives the 1.06:1 that is the bug. It is an `outline` and not
a `border`: a border grows the field by 2px on every focus, and an outline
follows `border-radius`, costs no layout, and is painted in Windows high-contrast
mode, where the `box-shadow` ring `button-boundary-0811` gave the buttons is not.
The border swap is kept as a second cue. `.modal` and `.main` both declare
`overflow: auto`, so a 2px-offset ring is a clipping risk — checked, not assumed:
both carry ≥28px padding.

Six controls (text, select, number, password, `.hl-new`, login) across four
screens in both colour schemes. 24/24, exit 0, twelve screenshots.

Three method notes, since this is the first run here grading a state change
rather than a resting colour. The clip is page coordinates expanded by 8px, not
`elementHandle.screenshot()` — that crops to the border box, which is exactly
where an outline is not. The surface under the ring is read from the pixel
actually painted in the unfocused shot, because with an offset the ring lands on
whatever is behind the control. And the grade is the best of three cues — ring,
border swap, fill swap — so a control cannot pass on a ring while the credit
belongs to the border, or fail while a visible fill change is on screen.

The twelve control rows re-inject `outline: none` and are required to come out
under 3:1; all twelve do. They are what makes this measurable: a pixel diff was
not enough, because the border and fill did move slightly before the fix, so
"did anything change" passed in both states and measured nothing.
